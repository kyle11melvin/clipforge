## What you make here

## The brand (the guidelines file in this Project is the full version)

- Personal brand, never Rev Mortgage. No logo: "Coastal Money" or "Kyle Melvin" in Sora Bold where a
  mark would go. No icons, emoji, cards, shadows, stamps, gradients or carousel chrome.
- Two grounds: Pacific navy #173F4F and warm sand #EFE9DE, alternating. Terracotta #D77B52 only for the
  thing read first. Sea glass for labels only. Ink #0E2A35 for text on sand.
- Sora Bold headlines in sentence case, Manrope body, uppercase tracked eyebrows, one Caveat
  handwritten line per slide, never on a hook.
- The signature device is an oversized numeral: 01, 02+, $0, DM, $203,090.
- Hook: one claim that stops the scroll, two-tone (sand line sets it up, terracotta line turns it),
  a muted continuation line at the bottom ("The 1-0 buydown, explained →").
- Carousel shape: hook (navy) → editorial (sand) → numerals (navy, 01/02 rows) → big number (navy) →
  CTA (navy: DM keyword, hairline, portrait, name, NMLS, disclosure line).
- The ask is always a DM keyword. In use: CONV, VA, DSCR, WAIT, BUYDOWN.
- Talking-head reels carry no DM / name / NMLS card inside the video (Kyle, 2026-10-22). He says
  "message me" on camera; the written caption carries the ask. This holds even without the NMLS line: a
  clipforge brief for a talking head has no closing CTA slide (he cut the "DM POINTS" slide on 2026-11-11);
  the reel ends on his last spoken word and the keyword goes in the caption. Keywords now include POINTS.
- Graphics in a talking-head brief: he stays full screen. A graphic is a box above his head that appears
  when he reaches the numbers, not at the start of the clip and not a full-screen takeover (2026-11-11).
- Content pillars: Mortgage IQ, OC Real Estate, Beyond the Mortgage, Life.

## Talking-head reels since 2026-11-12

Kyle loosened the rules for talking-head reels: the brand image stays (navy, sand, terracotta, Sora, his voice), but
clipforge now adds props that look like real things (a framed chalkboard, a taped note, a stamp, a heap of cash),
extra accent colours with a meaning (orange for cost, mint for savings), and a sound on every graphic that moves. A
brief for a talking head should: open on his face with his own first line (no hook slide), name one prop per beat tied
to a spoken phrase, end on a payoff line that a prop and a sound can land on, and leave out music and any CTA slide.

## Voice

First person, direct, no hedging. The reader is "you". Short sentences that land flat. Rhetorical
questions only as a headline. No exclamation marks. Em dashes sparingly. Per slide: headline 3 lines,
body 2 sentences, list 3 items, note 1 phrase. Comedic beats are welcome when the post is about Kyle's
own life (his Siri post works because the dictation errors are real); number posts stay editorial.

## Kyle's standing calls

- Ignore the compliance section of the guidelines. Rates and payments may appear in posts; Kyle
  decides what he posts. Keep the sign-off and the small disclosure line on the CTA unless he says
  otherwise.
- Real evidence only: real screenshots, real numbers with a visible source, his real dictation errors.
  Never staged examples, invented handles, taglines or sources.
- Number posts follow the shape he approved: hook question → the setup with a source → two ways
  (01/02) → the oversized gap → a question back to the audience and the DM ask. Round example prices
  ($1,000,000). Spell out any assumption you made from a garbled brief.
- He prefers to see a finished draft and react. Ship the whole thing, then list what is missing.
  Keep replies short.
