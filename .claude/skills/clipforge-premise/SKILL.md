# Premise and script

Nothing gets built until Kyle has picked a premise. He said it himself (2026-11-14): keep the graphics machine;
the ideas have to get better. `history.md` beside this file has the posts these rules came from.

## The reach formula

What travelled: Broncos meme (~15K views, 243 shares), SiriusXM text post (~300 likes, ~120 comments from
strangers). What did not: the polished family reel with a pitch (46 story views, 2 likes). So every premise has:

1. Something he actually did or saw.
2. One real number.
3. A question back to the audience ("am I the only one?").
4. No CTA in the post. The ask goes in a reply or the caption.

Brand graphics are for the grid and credibility, not for discovery.

## What "creative" means to him

An idea, not a format. A counter and a caption are decoration. Find the joke in the photo or the argument in the
situation and build around that. For a skit: a scripted joke with shot-reverse-shot, few words, held silences, a
location change for the punchline. Clipforge cannot generate actors; give him a script and shot list he can shoot.

## Steps

1. Read the newest two notes in `docs/log/` so the pitch is not a repeat of last week's shape or gag.
2. Pitch **three premises**, each in three lines: the thing he did or saw, the number, the question. Say which
   account (lender or personal) and which shape (talking head, meme, graphics-only, text post on Threads).
3. When he picks one, write the script in his voice: first person, plain talk, comedic not corporate, never
   preachy, real math in dollars. Short lines, one take each.
4. If graphics will follow his words, write lines that give them something to do: a spoken list ("three things the
   seller can pay for"), a count, a before and after, a number that changes. Mark those lines.
5. Give the shooting plan: the lines, one take each, a three-second reaction shot, and the framing the format needs
   (seated tripod shot with room beside his head for cards or paper-cut type; still and seated for a cutout).
   Ask for a cover photo in the same Drive folder.
6. Write the caption only if he has not sent his own: first person, one idea per paragraph, no emoji, no hashtags.
   His free two-minute home-buying quiz can be the ask ("Link is in the comments if you want it").

## Rules

- A missing line is re-shot or cut. A voice memo never passes for it.
- Facts and numbers come from a real source; say which. Round example prices, P&I only, "example, not a quote".
- Never name a loan program after a neighborhood (fair-lending problem).
- A brief from the Coastal Money Project is a first draft. The standing rules outrank it; say what was changed.
- Threads: plain text in his caption voice with a question; it ranks by replies. Lender account: Real Estate topic
  tag, reply to comments early.
- His messages are dictated. Confirm any wording that will appear on screen.
- Build his premise. Do not decorate it with a second joke of your own.
